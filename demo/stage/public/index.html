    Night Desk

    A journalist, an anonymous name, and a proof that it was her all along.

    EP

    Elena Petrova

    investigative reporter

    01 · Elena's app gets ready

    Every month, her app quietly collects 20 tokens.

    Each token lets her create one anonymous name. The server signs them blindfolded, so it will never recognise them later. Every reporter's app collects them, whether or not they ever use one.

    Elena's laptop

    Elena is signed in to her normal account, with her email and password.

    02 · Become anonymous

    Elena creates Night Desk.

    No email. No password. No name. Her laptop makes a new key, and twelve words are the only way back in.

    Elena's laptopsigned out

    1. Make twelve words and Night Desk's key, on this laptop
    2. Register Night Desk in the public directory
    3. Open the account, paying with one token

    Night Desk's twelve words · write them down

      03 · Look inside the server

      What does the server know?

      Straight from the server's database, not a slide.

      04 · Log in and publish

      Twelve words. That's the whole login.

      Night Desk signs in with the words alone, then publishes. A fingerprint of the article is kept in Night Desk's account, so it can't quietly be changed later.

      Sign insigned out

      05 · Prove it was me

      Months later, Elena needs to prove she is Night Desk.

      Someone in the room picks a word. Night Desk's words sign it, here and now. A separate verifier checks the signature against Night Desk's key in the public directory.

      What you just saw

      • An anonymous name, created in seconds, that the server can't connect to Elena.
      • A login with twelve words and nothing else.
      • A proof of authorship that only the real Night Desk can give.

      Next: Elena holds her own key too, so the server can't speak for her either. Open source, and built on OpenFederation.
